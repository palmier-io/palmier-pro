# Reference-driven geopolitical explainers

Analysis date: 2026-11-08. Reference: [How Malaysia is becoming the next global chip giant](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH5WptvpLCY), CaspianReport, published 2024-07-20, duration 20:34.

## Conclusion

This is a **researched, narration-led documentary with recurring explanatory maps**, not a stock montage with captions. Compositor Pro has useful editing and finishing primitives. The missing product layer is an evidence-backed production workflow that connects **claims → script → narration → scenes → graphics → review → delivery**.

Highest-value capabilities:

1. Understand a reference as an editorial recipe, not only a color look.
2. Preserve sources, numbers, dates, and factual uncertainty alongside the script.
3. Build scenes against measured narration and keep dependencies intact during revisions.
4. Author reusable geographic, data, source-card, and historical-timeline graphics.
5. Carry a long production through asynchronous work without continuation prompts.
6. Review factual alignment and temporal behavior, not just six stills and audio meters.

**Fewer user prompts do not mean fewer internal tool calls.** The app should own timing, placement, asset preparation, dependency tracking, and job completion. The model should choose the argument, evidence, scene purpose, and creative direction.

## 1. What the video actually does

### Narrative architecture

Exact boundaries below come from YouTube chapter metadata. They are editorial sections, not detected shot boundaries.

- **00:00–02:02 — Malaysia's tech boom.** Starts with an unusual development, stacks concrete investment examples, then turns optimism into a dilemma: industrial upgrading versus domestic weaknesses and geopolitical pressure. Ends the opening on a memorable high-stakes proposition. Visual samples pair location maps and company marks with city/industrial imagery, a ranking graphic, and an attributed investment headline.
- **02:02–03:37 — Odoo sponsorship.** A separate production mode: business footage, application screens, a guided website-building demonstration, and a sponsor card. This is an optional commercial segment, not a mandatory part of the explainer recipe.
- **03:37–06:32 — The quest for high value.** Explains the middle-income trap through a Malaysia/South Korea comparison, then introduces China-plus-one, semiconductor positioning, and industrial policy. Samples include a dark two-series economic chart, threshold annotation, logistics footage, maps, technology imagery, and named-firm archival material.
- **06:32–08:54 — Historical context.** Reframes the opportunity through earlier policy choices, redistribution, industrialization, research investment, and migration. Uses archival-window treatments, city footage, a historical growth chart, and geographic diagrams.
- **08:54–10:39 — The role of Penang.** Narrows the scale from country to industrial cluster, introduces location and firms, then explains the video's account of diversion toward the capital. Repeated locator boxes, company logos, and regional map views make this a spatial argument rather than generic narration coverage.
- **10:39–12:46 — Revitalizing the industry.** Moves among Penang, Kulim, ports, other peninsula hubs, and Borneo. Company marks and place labels accumulate in a shared geographic setting. Samples alternate broad context with detailed regional framing.
- **12:46–14:28 — Domestic challenges.** The optimism meets labor, wages, migration, and housing constraints. Uses a brain-drain map annotation, human/industrial coverage, and an attributed housing headline. The spoken domestic-problem discussion extends beyond the chapter boundary; chapter metadata alone is not a semantic segmentation system.
- **14:28–16:59 — Regional competition.** Compares Singapore, pay, industrial investment, and political stability. Samples show wage labels, an attributed migration statistic, city coverage, and a policy timeline with portraits. Thailand and Vietnam continue into the next metadata chapter.
- **16:59–20:34 — Geopolitical balancing act.** Develops regional competition, possible cooperation, sanctions exposure, and dependence on both China and the US. Maps communicate relationships; industrial/news imagery supports named claims; closing branding and credits complete the film.

The sponsor occupies 95 seconds, approximately 7.7% of the reference. Remaining runtime is 18:59, including branding and credits. Do not automatically reproduce advertising when matching the editorial style.

### Why the story holds together

- **One governing question:** can Malaysia convert an investment opening into lasting high-value industrial power?
- **Alternating promise and constraint:** investment → upgrading problem → historical explanation → successful cluster → new opportunity → domestic constraints → competitors → geopolitical trade-off.
- **Scale changes with the argument:** global capital, Southeast Asia, national policy, regional hubs, particular firms, individual political decisions.
- **Concrete evidence precedes abstraction:** company investments, locations, graphs, and wage comparisons give the viewer handles for larger claims.
- **The ending answers conditionally:** opportunity remains dependent on local capability and external relationships. It does not simply repeat the opening's optimistic headline.

An agent must preserve this causal structure. Selecting visually relevant footage alone will not produce the same kind of film.

## 2. Visual and editorial grammar

### A. Maps are the main explanatory environment

Observed samples use textured terrain, contrasting country fills, teal water, rectangular country labels, smaller place names, point markers, company logos, region boxes, and curved/dashed connections. Broad regional views recur between close regional explanations. Later samples add brain-drain labels and large directional arrows.

The important capability is **geographic semantics**, not a fixed aesthetic. Store actual locations, regions, relationships, and label anchors. When the camera changes, pins, logos, boundaries, and routes must stay attached to the same map coordinates. A reusable map scene should support successive reveals without rebuilding unrelated geometry.

Required behaviors:

- Resolve and disambiguate places; include island and mainland Malaysia, not an accidental peninsula-only map.
- Highlight countries and industrial regions; place named hubs and firms accurately.
- Choose a projection and retain a shared coordinate system across views.
- Reveal routes with meaning and direction; distinguish illustrative links from sourced quantitative flows.
- Move between region and hub framing while keeping labels legible and avoiding collisions.
- Bind reveals and camera holds to narration beats, with sufficient time to understand them.
- Preserve data and style separately so a factual correction does not require redrawing everything.

### B. Data scenes explain comparisons

Samples show a two-series economic comparison, an annotated middle-income region, a Malaysia growth chart, rank information, and geographic wage labels. Different camera framings of the same chart suggest progressive explanation; exact movement is unverified.

An agent needs typed data scenes: values, series, units, dates, source, labels, annotations, and reveal order. It must distinguish GDP from GDP per capita, growth from level, nominal from adjusted dollars, and announced from realized investment. A chart cannot be treated as an AI-generated decorative picture.

### C. Source cards create evidence beats

Observed source cards use a restrained dark background, publication name, compact headline/quotation, date, and highlighted phrase. These are not ordinary subtitles. Their purpose is to pause the visual stream and support a particular assertion.

Source cards need an exact quotation or clearly marked paraphrase, a retrievable source reference, attribution, date, and a readable hold. A source-backed-looking card with an invented publication or number is worse than missing coverage.

### D. Historical and news material have a separate treatment

Archival samples sit inside rounded windows with grain, dark surrounds, small technical markings, and occasional warm edge coloration. Modern city and industrial coverage often fills the canvas instead. Policy scenes use portraits on dated event graphics.

These treatments separate modes of evidence. They should be reusable scene treatments, not dozens of unrelated manual effects. Do not fabricate a historical image, impersonate news footage, or silently imply generic factory footage depicts a named company.

### E. Typography has roles

Country labels, geographic place names, chapter cards, figure labels, source cards, and credits have distinct jobs. Samples suggest spaced uppercase labels, smaller map typography, and restrained dark-panel text. Exact font families and numeric style values were not recoverable from these thumbnails.

Use a coherent original hierarchy. Do not replace this language with continuous social-style word captions by default. Captions can be an accessibility deliverable without becoming the dominant visual layer.

### F. Pacing follows comprehension, not a beat grid

The same map and chart environments recur over multiple consecutive samples. New facts change the visual focus; occasional footage gives a break from dense explanatory graphics. Chapter cards reset the argument.

Do not infer exact cut frequency from ten-second sampling. A proposed recipe should hold unfamiliar maps and comparisons longer than simple establishing footage, reveal one idea at a time, and cut around complete spoken thoughts. These are recommendations, not measured duration rules from this video.

### G. Sound needs a separate assessment

Captions establish extensive voiceover and music markers near the ending, but do not reveal bed levels, musical changes, EQ, sound effects, speech intelligibility, or mix quality. Required product capability is sustained, consistent narration with appropriate pauses, pronunciation, seam handling, restrained music, and completed-output listening/QC. No voice clone or exact soundtrack replication is necessary.

The ending credits explicitly separate production, research, copy editing, and mapping/motion graphics. That is a useful summary of the roles an autonomous system must cover.

- **Footage retrieval:** library visual/spoken evidence and remote catalog evidence in [ShotCandidates.swift](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Retrieval/ShotCandidates.swift). Search, inspection, import, and source-span placement should be reused.
- **Local voiceover:** voice discovery and speech generation/import/optional placement in [ToolExecutor+Speech.swift](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolExecutor+Speech.swift). This is narration generation, not a persistent script-to-scene dependency system.
- **Visual primitives:** text styles, layouts, clip keyframes, transitions, color looks, image composition, and four clip-motion presets. [ToolExecutor+MotionPreset.swift:12–17](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolExecutor+MotionPreset.swift#L12) exposes fadeIn, slideUp, punchIn, and gentlePush; these are not map-route or chart-series animation.
- **Animation substrate:** local Lottie JSON import is implemented in [ToolExecutor+Import.swift:83–89](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolExecutor+Import.swift#L83), with alpha ProRes baking in [LottieVideoGenerator.swift](../Sources/CompositorPro/Preview/LottieVideoGenerator.swift). Therefore complex graphics are not fundamentally impossible today; a coding-capable client could author external assets. That does not make them a dependable one-brief editor feature.
- **Finishing:** encoded review, audio metrics, export waiting, and shared prepared audio exist in the current working changes. See [local-finishing.md](local-finishing.md). This audit does not reclassify previous audio defects as still missing without testing the updated source.
- **Reusable creative instructions:** existing skills can carry an explainer recipe. A recipe can guide decisions but cannot supply missing deterministic operations or durable job state.

### Immediate contract problems

1. **Assembly is misadvertised.** [ToolDefinitions.swift:323–327](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolDefinitions.swift#L323) says `apply_plan` performs no timeline edits and only reserves readiness. The executor assembles clips. This can make the model stop prematurely or add clips manually after an already successful assembly. Correct descriptions and test discovery against execution before adding another assembly tool.
2. **Lottie import is under-advertised.** [ToolDefinitions.swift:380](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolDefinitions.swift#L380) omits it from supported types despite implementation support. Make documented acquisition paths match actual paths.
3. **Shot-candidate metadata is over-advertised.** [ToolDefinitions.swift:1557](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolDefinitions.swift#L1557) promises dimension metadata, but [ShotCandidate.receipt](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Retrieval/ShotCandidates.swift#L51) does not return dimensions or thumbnails. Reliable shot selection needs inspectable evidence and honest receipts.
4. **Long-form defaults conflict with this request class.** [AgentInstructions.swift:17–20](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/AgentInstructions.swift#L17) defaults to 30–60 seconds. A reference-driven explainer requires explicit format/duration intent. Infer style separately from runtime; ask one question if “like this” could mean a short adaptation or a full-length documentary.
5. **Continuation is uneven.** Local review/export can be awaited, but [AgentInstructions.swift:272–277](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/AgentInstructions.swift#L272) still instructs the caller to ask the user to continue after long generation/import-related work. The source does not expose a persisted whole-production run.

These are source-observed mismatches, not runtime reproductions. Do not count fixing a description as implementing a new renderer.

## 4. Capabilities needed for fewer prompts

### P0 — Reference and research become usable production inputs

**Reference recipe.** Return format, narrative arc, scene families, visual hierarchy, pacing intent, map/chart usage, evidence treatment, audio observations, and inspection coverage. Distinguish observations from guesses. Separate transferable editorial language from channel logos, exact wording, protected footage, and unavailable audio evidence.

**Research and claim ledger.** Keep stable claim IDs with source references, publication/access dates, exact passages, numerical values/units, confidence or unresolved status, and links to script paragraphs and on-screen graphics. External agents can use their existing research tools; Compositor Pro needs durable evidence handoff, not necessarily a built-in browser. An in-app agent needs an explicit acquisition path or user-supplied research. Merely attaching a stock license is not factual verification.

**Narration-first scene plan.** Add stable paragraph/beat IDs and narration anchors to the existing plan. Describe each scene's editorial purpose and evidence requirement before assigning media. Generate or import narration, measure it, then resolve scene timing through shared domain operations. Do not make the model guess every frame from word count.

**One grouped approval when needed.** Collect consequential blockers—missing research, commercial assets, voice requirements, spending limits—into one review, rather than questions about every shot. For publication-sensitive journalism, approving the sourced script is a sensible checkpoint; exact autonomy level is a user choice.

### P1 — Semantic graphics and dependency-aware revisions

**Map scenes.** Given places, regions, firms, relations, and narration anchors, create a deterministic, editable geographic scene. Own projection, camera path, geographic placement, reveal order, label layout, and styling in one feature. Static PNG plus unrelated keyframes is insufficient for maintaining location attachments during revisions.

**Data and evidence scenes.** Support a small initial family: comparison line chart, numeric comparison, source card, and dated policy timeline. Store data and citation separately from styling and animation. Calculate scales and label positions deterministically; reject invalid units, missing data, and unsupported combinations.

**Verified coverage acquisition.** Find a bounded shortlist, inspect actual imagery, import suitable licensed assets, wait for readiness, and attach provenance. Classify material as direct evidence, contextual illustration, archival evidence, or explicit diagram. Generic footage may illustrate an industry; it must not impersonate a specific factory or prove a location.

**Dependency-aware revisions.** “Shorten the Penang explanation” should update affected narration, scene timings, map reveals, source cards, music, downstream section positions, and subtitles together. Preserve unrelated sections and stable scene IDs. Generated timing is derived from authored beats and measured narration, not a second editable source of truth. Surface conflicts when the user has manually overridden derived timing.

**Editable graphics assets.** Preserve original specifications/data alongside rendered media in the project. A baked movie can provide playback/export, but revising a label or figure must regenerate only the affected scene. Reuse Lottie where it satisfies the required contract; do not blindly write large raw JSON with the LLM for every production.

### P1 — An application-owned production lifecycle

Build one production coordinator around existing plan, mutation, package, and export services. It owns brief, project/timeline identity, source revisions, current phase, decisions, job handles, coverage, cancellation, and terminal receipt. Store authored content in its owning plan/graphic artifacts rather than duplicating it in the run.

Flow:

```text
brief + reference + sources + budget
                  ↓
reference recipe → sourced argument → script approval, if required
                  ↓
narration → measured beats → resolved scene plan
                  ↓
verified coverage + semantic graphics
                  ↓
existing atomic assembly → finishing → review → bounded repairs
                  ↓
export completion → output verification → deliverable + source/rights manifest
```

- Job completion resumes the matching run; no busy-polling or user “continue” messages.
- Persist enough state to survive closing and reopening; re-resolve projects/assets before resuming.
- Every awaited result is checked against cancellation, current project, source identity, and revision before commit.
- Bound downloads, decoders, graphics preparation, speech, and rendering separately according to measured resource limits.
- Keep file/media work off the main actor and live package mutations behind `ProjectPackageCoordinator`.
- Do not hold an AppKit undo group across background waits. Commit short coherent edits; one section revision should undo as one user action without absorbing unrelated edits.
- Return completed, canceled, failed, or explicitly blocked outcomes, including partial work and unresolved warnings.
- In-app execution can own continuation. External MCP execution must expose durable state/waitable completion; it cannot assume every external client auto-resumes a conversation.

### P2 — Review, quality control, and efficient context

Current [review_timeline](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Tools/ToolExecutor+ReviewTimeline.swift#L16) limits each render to 60 seconds. [RenderedTimelineReview.swift:27–36](../Sources/CompositorPro/Preview/RenderedTimelineReview.swift#L27) samples six frames and separately measures audio. That is useful but insufficient to certify a twenty-minute map documentary. Reviewing the full reference length in maximum-size windows would require at least 21 review renders; six samples per minute still leave most temporal behavior unseen.

Required review layers:

- **Structural:** missing media, exact timing, unintended blank intervals, orphaned graphics, and delivery settings.
- **Factual:** claim matches narration, displayed number, chart units, source date, geographic location, and material classification.
- **Temporal:** label reveals, camera/route movement, graphic comprehension holds, speech seams, and chapter transitions. Use short moving ranges around dense changes, not only uniformly sampled stills.
- **Audio:** final-mix loudness, clipping, speech/bed relationship, narration completeness; keep true peak distinct from sample peak.
- **Coverage:** requested intervals, successfully analyzed intervals, failures, and unverified modalities. A still-only model must not claim it watched motion or listened.
- **Repair:** bounded attempts, evidence-linked changes, unaffected-section preservation, and truthful remaining issues.

Maintain project-wide constraints and section-local working context. [AgentService.swift:538–565](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Chat/AgentService.swift#L538) currently sends the complete in-app tool set and reconstructed conversation into its loop; [665–689](../Sources/CompositorPro/Agent/Chat/AgentService.swift#L665) executes tool calls serially. Task-relevant discovery, compact state receipts, and bounded independent preparation can reduce overhead, but latency/token improvements require comparable measurements. Do not parallelize conflicting editor mutations.

The [current Lottie baking path](../Sources/CompositorPro/Preview/LottieVideoGenerator.swift#L181) also performs frame rendering and writer orchestration from a main-actor method. Treat it as a substrate requiring responsiveness, cancellation, memory, and cache-invalidation verification before scaling graphics-heavy long productions—not proof that a twenty-minute motion workflow is already safe.

## 5. Tool design: fewer commands at the right level

Illustrative intent-level operations, not implemented tools:

- **Produce an explainer:** accept topic/material, reference recipe, intended duration/platform, evidence policy, voice preference, and spending limit; return a production-run ID or completed deliverable. This is one coherent workflow, not a multi-mode god tool.
- **Build a map scene:** accept geographic entities/relationships, scene purpose, narration anchors, and style; return editable scene and assets with evidence references.
- **Build a data scene:** accept a typed explanatory graphic, sourced data, annotations, and narration anchors; return editable scene and assets. Reuse text/layout/motion machinery internally rather than requiring the agent to construct every label.
- **Revise a section:** accept stable section ID and editorial change; return affected scene IDs, timing changes, no-op status, warnings, and undo semantics.
- **Inspect production:** return phase, owned jobs, blockers, coverage, unresolved issues, and terminal output. Provide explicit cancellation and wait semantics.

The full workflow can be one user request while using many internal domain operations. Keep existing narrow editing tools available for surgical work. Do not replace them with an opaque renderer or duplicate the editor's placement math.

## 6. Smallest useful release and rollout

### First: a 90-second vertical slice

Build a landscape explainer from supplied, verified research, with one narrator, one locator/route map, one sourced comparison, contextual footage, consistent typography, and no sponsor. Require one brief through a playable, reviewed export. This tests the unique capabilities without pretending a montage benchmark proves documentary readiness.

Fix discovery/contract mismatches first. Reuse current local finishing. Add the smallest source-linked script/scene model, a limited semantic graphic family, and an owned production lifecycle. Expand only after this slice completes reliably.

### Next: a 3–5-minute chaptered explainer

Exercise shared map reuse, more than one region, pronunciation corrections, a changed statistic, section shortening, a failed asset, and close/reopen. Add sourced external research only with an explicit acquisition and verification contract.

### Then: an 18–21-minute documentary

Benchmark long-context continuity, section-level regeneration, full-output coverage, memory/cache bounds, graphics responsiveness, and production recovery. Do not claim parity with this reference before that workload is measured and played back.

## 7. Proposed acceptance criteria

These are targets, not achieved results.

- One complete brief; zero continuation prompts; at most one grouped clarification/approval when required inputs are incomplete or publication policy requires it.
- Every factual on-screen number and named location links to a reviewed source/claim. Every borrowed asset has rights metadata. Current reporting does not reuse 2024 figures without rechecking them.
- Narration, source cards, chart values, and captions agree. Correcting a statistic updates dependent outputs without leaving the old figure elsewhere.
- Shortening one section retimes dependent elements and downstream sections, preserves unrelated content, and undoes exactly.
- Cancellation, failed download, stale source, user edit, duplicated job completion, close/reopen, and export failure produce accurate recoverable or terminal states.
- Read state back through MCP and inspect the output independently; success receipts alone are not evidence of completion.
- Verify graph location/reveal behavior with continuous playback, caption/source-card readability at delivery resolution, all audio seams, opening, ending, and exact delivery settings.
- Record user messages, internal tool calls, model tokens, time to first playable cut, time to verified export, repair count, peak memory, factual defects, and manual interventions under fixed comparable briefs.

Manual UI plan: create an isolated project with supplied research, licensed coverage, and a known narrator. Run the 90-second brief, inspect full playback with sound, revise one section, undo it, cancel a new run, and repeat after close/reopen. Expect stable unrelated sections, accurate evidence, attached geographic labels, legible holds, completed export, and no silent continuation dependency. UI success remains unverified until a person confirms it.

## Example brief

> Make a 5-minute, 16:9 YouTube explainer about Malaysia's semiconductor strategy using this video as an editorial reference, not a source to copy. Use my supplied research and licensed footage. Build a clear opportunity-versus-constraints argument with accurate maps, sourced comparisons, selective archival coverage, restrained typography, and consistent narration. Keep claims dated and traceable. No sponsor, no channel-logo copying, and no paid generation. Ask only for consequential blockers; assemble, finish, review, repair, and deliver the completed export with sources and asset credits.

This is a **target product experience**, not a promise the current app can reliably complete from that prompt today.
